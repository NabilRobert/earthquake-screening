# Slice 2: a building-level surrogate on PRJ-2048, with its pass rule

**Data:** Guan, Burton and Shokrabadi (2020), DesignSafe PRJ-2048
([doi:10.17603/ds2-8yc7-1285](https://doi.org/10.17603/ds2-8yc7-1285), ODC-By).
The dataset has 621 steel moment frames, each simulated in OpenSees under the same
240 ground motions. We use three parts of it: the design summary, the peak storey
drifts and the ground-motion histories. None of it is redistributed.

**Question:** given a frame's design and the shaking, how well does a fast model
predict the frame's **peak inter-storey drift**, the largest over its storeys? We
judge it on frames and ground motions it has never seen, against the full
simulation's answer.

## Fixed before any run (2026-11-03)

Before this was written, we had looked only at the files' layout and the spread of
the raw values: drift ratios from 0.00006 to 0.38, with a median of 0.003, and peak
ground accelerations from 0.03 to 0.78 g. No model had been fitted. Nothing below will
be tuned against the result. Every run is logged in the result section.

### Inputs and output

| | What | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Building, 6 inputs | storeys (1, 5, 9, 14 or 19), bay width (ft), PRJ-2048's "story height ratio", number of bays, floor dead load (psf), roof dead load (psf) | `DesignSummary.csv` |
| Shaking, 6 inputs | PGA, plus the 5%-damped pseudo-spectral acceleration Sa at 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 s, all in g, used as logarithms | Computed from each history with the exact linear solver used for slice 1's oscillator |
| Output | ln(peak inter-storey drift ratio), the largest over storeys | `PeakStoryDrift/Building_<id>_PSDR.csv` |

**Dropped:** the column-to-beam ratio, because it is 1.0 for every frame. The shaking
is described by a response spectrum because that is what a hazard tool gives a
user; the frames' periods aren't in the files we fetched. The histories are taken to
be in g: the file headers don't say, but the range of peak values (0.03 to 0.78) only
makes sense in g.

**Collapses** are kept as results, not removed (requirement S3). The one drift of
0.38 stays in the data.

### The split (seed 2048), by building and by ground motion

- **Buildings:** 20% of frames for **test**, 15% for **calibration** and 65% for
  **fit**. The split is stratified by storey count, so every height appears in each
  part. No frame is in two parts.
- **Ground motions:** 48 of the 240 (20%) are **held out**. The fit never sees them.

| Rows | Buildings | Ground motions | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fit | fit | the 192 others | Training and choosing the model |
| Calibration | calibration | the 48 held out | The conformal intervals and the refusal limit |
| **Test (judged)** | test | the 48 held out | The pass rule: frames and shaking both unseen |
| Test, known shaking (reported) | test | the 192 others | New frames under shaking seen in training |

Calibration and test both pair unseen frames with unseen ground motions, so their
errors are exchangeable, which conformal coverage needs. A test proves the split:
no frame is in two parts, and no held-out ground motion is in the fit rows.

### The model

Two candidates are compared by 5-fold cross-validation **on the fit rows only**,
grouped by building. The lower mean RMSE of ln drift wins. A tie goes to boosting.

- **Gradient boosting:** scikit-learn's `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`, 400
  iterations, learning rate 0.05, no tuning.
- **Gaussian process:** scikit-learn's `GaussianProcessRegressor`, a constant times
  RBF kernel with one length per input, plus a white-noise kernel, on standardised
  inputs. A GP costs n³, so it trains on a random 2,000-row sample. That is the
  largest that fits a laptop comfortably, and the cap is part of the comparison.

The winner is refitted on all fit rows.

### Intervals

MAPIE's `SplitConformalRegressor` gives 90% intervals with the absolute-residual
score on ln drift. It is calibrated on the calibration rows. In drift, each interval
is the prediction times or divided by a single factor.

### The baseline: a class table, Hazus-style

Hazus groups steel moment frames (S1) into **low (1–3 storeys), mid (4–7) and high
(8+)**. Our frames fall into those classes as follows: 1 storey is low, 5 is mid, and
9, 14 and 19 are high. For each class, the baseline fits one line, ln drift = a + b ×
ln Sa(T), on the same fit rows. T is the one period from the five that fits that
class best on the fit rows. It ignores everything about a frame except its class,
which is what a class table does.

**Why not pelicun's Hazus data:** pelicun's damage-and-loss library (DLML) holds
Hazus's damage-state drift limits and intensity-based fragilities, not drift-demand
curves, so it can't predict a drift. pelicun 3.10 also requires scipy below 1.16,
and slice 1 is locked to 1.18. The damage-state limits are for slice 4, read from
DLML directly.

### Refusing outside the training range

The training envelope is saved with the model:
- **Each input's range:** an input below its fit-row minimum or above its fit-row
  maximum is refused, and the reason names the input, the value and the range.
  Non-numbers are refused.
- **Combinations:** the distance to the nearest fit row, in standardised inputs,
  must be at most the 99th percentile of the calibration rows' distances. The limit
  comes from calibration, never from test.

Refused inputs get a reason, never a number. The full probe suite is slice 3.

### The pass rule

All three are judged on the **test rows**, where both the frames and the shaking are
unseen, with **no refusals applied**, so refusing can't flatter the numbers:

1. **Split (M1):** the split test passes.
2. **Coverage (G2, ADR-008):** the 90% intervals cover between **88% and 92%** of
   the test rows.
3. **Better than the baseline (G3):** the model's RMSE of ln drift is lower than the
   class table's on the same rows.

**PASS** means all three hold. Anything else is a **miss** and is reported as one.

**Reported, not judged:**
- RMSE of ln drift, the median of |predicted ÷ simulated − 1|, and the share within a
  factor of 1.5 and of 2;
- the median interval width, as a factor;
- coverage and error by storey count;
- the same numbers for test rows with known shaking, and for test rows the envelope
  accepts;
- the refusal rate;
- both candidates' cross-validation scores;
- the time per prediction.

**A caution, written before the run:** about 124 test frames and 48 ground motions
make about 6,000 rows, but rows from one frame or one record are correlated. The
2-point coverage band is therefore tighter than the row count suggests. A miss
would still be a miss.

**Time budget (G6):** the whole run, from the files to the report, stays under
30 minutes on the laptop at $0. The model file's SHA-256 is recorded with the metrics
(M5).

## Result: PASS (run once, 2026-11-03)

Full tables: [prj2048-result.md](prj2048-result.md). The run used commit f906a35 and
took 5.7 min on the laptop, at $0. It was run once, with nothing changed after
seeing it.

| Judged on the test rows (6,000: 125 unseen frames × 48 unseen ground motions) | Result | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Split clean | yes | must hold |
| 90% interval coverage | **90.6%** | 88–92% |
| RMSE of ln drift: gradient boosting vs class baseline | **0.290** vs 0.431 | lower than the baseline |

- **Accuracy:** the median error is 19%. 84% of predictions are within a factor of 1.5
  of the simulation, and 98% within a factor of 2. **The misses:** 1.9% of test rows
  are off by more than a factor of 2, and 9.4% fall outside their interval.
- **The choice:** gradient boosting beat the GP in cross-validation, 0.200 against
  0.252. The GP saw only 2,000 rows, so this is a comparison at a laptop's budget,
  not of the methods in general.

**What the PASS doesn't cover, found in the same run:**
- **Coverage is only right on average.** The 1-storey frames are covered at 82.6%,
  below the band, and the 14-storey frames at 93.7%, above it. Each interval has the
  same width, a factor of 1.62, whatever the frame. A frame-dependent interval
  (MAPIE's conformalized quantile regression, or a residual-scaled score) is the fix
  to try in a later slice. Like any later change, it would need a new pass rule
  fixed before running.
- **Intervals are too wide for shaking seen in training.** Test frames under the
  192 training ground motions are covered at 98.2%. The intervals were sized for
  unseen shaking, which is the harder case.
- **Refusals are 8.3% of test rows, not about 1%.** All 500 refused rows come from
  4 of the 48 held-out ground motions. Their Sa at 1 s or 2 s is stronger than any
  training motion's, so the range check refuses them. The distance check refused
  none. The surrogate's error on those rows (RMSE about 0.32) is only a little worse
  than on the rest, but refusing them is the rule (ADR-009). The 1% target in
  ADR-009 assumed in-range inputs.
- **Scope:** steel moment frames of 1 to 19 storeys, designed to one US code
  procedure, under 240 recorded motions, with drifts from one OpenSees model per
  frame. Concrete, masonry, irregular or older buildings are outside it. So is any
  check against real damage, and any claim of safety.
